How to use this pack

This is a working toolkit, not legal advice

Everything in this pack is a template, checklist or register. It is written to be practical and to reflect how small commercial sailing operations are usually run — but it is not legal advice and it does not replace the rules that actually bind you.

  • The flag state of the vessel decides which licence, coding, crewing and equipment rules apply.
  • The coastal state where you sail decides whether you may charter there at all.
  • Your insurer decides what is covered — and a licence that does not match the charter you are selling is the fastest way to void a policy.
  • Have the guest-facing documents (Section 2 and 3) reviewed by a lawyer qualified in Spanish consumer law before you use them with paying guests.

Three rules for using the pack

  1. Fill the blanks. Anything in square brackets — [legal name], [NIF], [registered address] — is a placeholder you must complete.
  2. Keep the signed originals. A waiver that exists but was never signed is worth nothing. Store signed copies with the trip file (Section 9).
  3. Verify locally, every season. Country rules in Section 8 change. Confirm them with the local maritime authority or charter agent before the first departure each year.
Priority action before the 2027 season. The Privacy Policy on the website still carries a note that the business is not yet registered, and there is no legal name, NIF or registered address anywhere in the published documents. Until that is fixed, the booking terms, the privacy policy and every invoice are incomplete. This is action number one in Section 1.

Section 01

Operating model & who is responsible

Before a single form is signed, decide in which capacity the trip is sold. The licence, the insurance and the liability follow the capacity — not the brand on the website.

1.1 The three ways a trip can be sold

ModelWho contracts with the guestVessel, flag & licenceInsurance that responds
A. Operator We do — we are the carrier and the organiser. The vessel must be commercially registered and licensed in its flag state for charter work. Commercial charter insurance in our name: hull & machinery plus P&I.
B. Organiser with a local operator We do, but the boat and crew come from a licensed local charter company. Their vessel, their flag, their charter licence. We must be named as an authorised reseller or intermediary. Theirs responds on board; ours responds for our own organising activity.
C. Retailer / seat on a licensed boat The licensed operator (for example, a cabin charter out of Phuket). Theirs entirely. Theirs. We hold professional indemnity and public liability for our own advice and marketing.
Why this page matters most. The moment you market and sell a trip as a package in your own name, EU consumer law (the Package Travel Directive, 2015/2302) can treat you as the organiser — with information duties and an insolvency-protection obligation. If you only introduce a guest to a licensed operator, say so clearly, everywhere, or you risk being treated as the organiser anyway.

1.2 Decide and record, before the season

  • Choose the model (A, B or C) for each trip and write it in the trip file.
  • Agree who issues the guest's invoice, in which currency and from which entity.
  • Confirm whose insurance responds if a guest is injured on board.
  • Confirm who is the data controller for the guest's booking data.
  • Confirm who holds the charter licence for the country of operation.
  • Confirm who is liable if the trip is cancelled at the last minute.

1.4 Consumer law: is it a package?

A charter on its own is usually a single travel service. It becomes a package when two or more different travel services are combined for the same trip — for example the charter plus flights, plus a hotel night, plus a transfer, sold together or billed together.

  • If we sell only the charter and the guest books their own flights, record that decision in writing.
  • If we arrange anything else as well, assume the package rules apply and provide the standard information form before booking.
  • If we take money for another supplier and pass it on, check the "linked travel arrangement" rules.
  • Note that the 14-day right of withdrawal does not apply to leisure services on a specific date — a charter is one. Say so in the terms so nobody asks for it.

  • Rules on board: alcohol, smoking, sun, hydration
  • Tender use and boarding rules
  • Swimming and snorkelling rules and buddy system
  • Waste, water use and marine-park rules
  • Emergency contacts and the daily plan

4.2 Emergency action plan — quick cards

ScenarioImmediate actions
Man overboardShout "man overboard", throw flotation, press MOB on the plotter, assign a spotter who does not lose sight, bring the boat back under engine, recover on the leeward side, treat for hypothermia, call Mayday if not recovered fast.
FireRaise the alarm, cut the fuel and gas, fight only small fires with the correct extinguisher, move guests upwind and to the foredeck, prepare to abandon ship, Mayday.
Medical emergencyFirst aid, call Mayday or the coastguard for medical advice, identify the nearest hospital and the fastest route, keep the guest's declaration and medication list with them.
Abandon shipMayday with position, lifejackets on, grab bag and water, launch the liferaft, board from the leeward side, stay tethered to the raft, do not swim away from the vessel.
Severe weatherReef early, brief guests, secure loose gear, lifejackets on, decide whether to run for shelter or heave to, monitor forecasts continuously.
FloodingLocate the source, use the bilge pumps and manual pump, plug what can be plugged, Mayday, head for the nearest safe haven.
Collision / groundingCheck all persons, sound the alarm, assess hull damage, notify the coastguard, do not continue if the integrity of the hull is in doubt.
Security / piracyFollow the flag-state and local guidance, do not resist, alert the authorities, keep guests below and calm.

5.2 Insurance — what the policy must actually cover

  • Hull & machinery — the vessel itself, including tender and toys.
  • P&I / third-party liability — injury to guests and crew, and damage to others.
  • Commercial charter use explicitly named. A private policy will not respond.
  • Geographic limits — confirm every sea area you sail in is inside the cruising range.
  • Crew cover — including the skipper and any paid host.
  • Water sports and tender liability, if you offer them.
  • Professional indemnity for the organising and marketing side of the business.
  • Certificates for the flag — a copy on board, in the language the port expects.

Section 07

Hosts, facilitators & creators

The collaboration model — a free berth and a fee in exchange for bringing a group — creates three documents and one legal duty that most people forget.

7.1 Host / facilitator agreement — what it must state

ClauseContent
Parties & capacityWho the host is, and that they are a guest or an invited collaborator — not crew, and not our employee.
Week & berthThe exact dates, the boat, and that the host's berth is complimentary.
Minimum groupThe number of paid guests the host must bring for the week to run, and what happens if they do not reach it.
Host's feeThat the host sets their own retail price for their guests and keeps 100% of the difference above our rate. Confirm in writing that we never touch it.
PaymentsWho collects from the guests, when, and how the balance reaches us.
DeliverablesThe content agreed — posts, reels, stories — and by when.
Advertising disclosureThe host must label paid or incentivised promotion clearly. See 7.3.
Image rightsWhat we may reuse, where, for how long, and with what credit.
ConductThe code of conduct in 7.4 applies to the host as well as the guests.
CancellationWhat happens if the host, the guests or we cancel, and who keeps what.
InsuranceThe host confirms their own travel and medical insurance.
Data protectionIf the host collects guests' details, they are a controller for that data and must handle it lawfully.
Governing lawWhich law applies and where disputes are heard.

7.2 Creator / influencer collaboration — additional points

  • State whether the berth is consideration for the content — if it is, the content is advertising.
  • Agree the deliverables as numbers, not adjectives: two reels, five stories, one post.
  • Agree when content may be published and for how long we may reuse it.
  • Include a morality clause: content that damages the brand can end the agreement.
  • Confirm the creator will not present themselves as our employee or agent.
  • Confirm the audience is genuine — check for bought followers before agreeing value.
7.3 The duty everyone forgets: label the advertising. A free berth given in exchange for promotion, or a payment, is advertising. Under the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive and Spain's advertising law, hidden advertising is an unfair practice — and the platform rules are stricter still. The host must use the platform's paid partnership / branded content label and a clear written disclosure (for example, "#ad" or "ad — free trip"), in a place a reader sees before they engage. Put this obligation in the agreement, and check it was done.

7.4 Code of conduct — hosts and guests

  • Respect for crew, other guests, local people and the marine environment.
  • No harassment, discrimination or aggression of any kind. Breach means removal at the next port, without refund.
  • Follow the skipper's instructions on safety, always and without argument.
  • Photography: respect the media-consent choices of others, and never film someone who has opted out.
  • Alcohol in moderation, and never while operating any equipment.
  • No illegal substances on board at any time.
  • Leave every anchorage cleaner than you found it.
Section 08

Country-by-country checklists

Chartering for reward is licensed by the coastal state. These are the items to verify locally, for the destinations on the 2027 calendar. Rules change — confirm each one with the local maritime authority or a local charter agent before the first departure.

Read this before using any row below. What follows is a working checklist of the categories that usually apply, not a statement of the current law of each country. In several of these jurisdictions a foreign-flagged yacht may not charter commercially at all without a local licence — which is why a licensed local operator is sometimes the only route.

Practical consequence for the 2027 season. For Thailand, the compliant structure is model C: a licensed Thai operator holds the vessel, the licence and the insurance, and we sell alongside them. Make sure the guest's contract, invoice and insurance certificate name the right carrier — not us.
