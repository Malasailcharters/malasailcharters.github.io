Compliance Pack — condensed

What you must actually have

The 25-page pack, reduced to the essentials. Everything here is a template, checklist or register — not legal advice. The flag state, the coastal state and your insurer each have a veto, so verify locally every season.

Do these twelve first. Numbers 1 to 5 are what actually stop you trading.
  • 1. Register the business. Legal name, NIF, registered address — then put them in the Terms, the Privacy Policy and every invoice. Right now they are missing.
  • 2. Decide, per trip, who contracts with the guest — you as operator, you as organiser, or the licensed operator as carrier. Write it in the trip file.
  • 3. Confirm whose insurance responds if a guest is injured on board — named in the policy, not assumed.
  • 4. Sort the charter licence for the country you actually sail in. Thailand: a licensed local operator with a Thai-registered vessel, or you do not charter there.
  • 5. Check the vessel policy names commercial charter use and covers every sea area on the calendar.
  • 6. Check the captain's file is in date — licence, STCW, medical, radio certificate.
  • 7. Fix the Terms: state a minimum guest insurance, whether the trip is a package, and the ADR route.
  • 8. Start using four guest forms: information, health declaration, risk acknowledgement, media consent.
  • 9. Print the safety briefing sheet and the risk assessment for every departure.
  • 10. Put the advertising-disclosure duty in every host and creator agreement (see page 3).
  • 11. Keep the signed originals. A waiver that exists but was never signed is worth nothing.
  • 12. Re-verify every country row before the first departure of the season.

The essentialsTen areas, and what each one needs

AreaYou must hold, or doDone
1 · Operating modelA written decision per trip on who contracts with the guest, and therefore whose licence, insurance and data-controller role applies.
2 · Customer documentsTerms, Privacy Policy, cookie notice, cancellation policy, complaints/ADR route — each carrying legal name, NIF, address, a minimum insurance requirement, and package status if applicable.
3 · Guest formsBooking agreement · guest information form · health declaration · signed risk acknowledgement · media consent (opt in/out) · parental consent for under-18s.
4 · Safety managementPre-departure briefing sheet · emergency action cards (MOB, fire, medical, abandon ship, weather, flooding) · risk assessment register · daily checks · incident and near-miss log.
5 · Vessel documentsCertificate of registry · charter licence · seaworthiness/coding · tonnage · ship radio licence · insurance certificate · LSA/FFE servicing · crew list · charter agreement · transit log where required.
6 · InsuranceHull & machinery · P&I / third-party · commercial charter use named · geographic limits covering every sea area · crew cover · water-sports and tender liability · professional indemnity for the organising side.
7 · EnvironmentMARPOL discipline: nothing over the side, garbage sorted and landed, shore pump-out for sewage, correct fuel, no anchoring in protected zones. Garbage log kept.
8 · CrewCaptain 200GT · STCW certificate of competency · STCW basic safety training · medical fitness in date · GMDSS/SRC · first aid · employment status decided in writing.
9 · Hosts & creatorsWritten agreement: free berth, minimum seats sold, the host's own fee kept in full, deliverables, cancellation, image rights, conduct. Plus advertising disclosure. Plus the code of conduct applied to hosts too.
10 · RecordsRenewal calendar · document register (where each original lives) · per-departure trip file · GDPR records of processing · annual review before the season.
Two documents decide most disputes. The signed risk acknowledgement (the guest understood the risks and accepted the skipper's authority) and the insurance certificate that actually names commercial charter use. If either is missing or out of date, everything else is decoration.

Four things people forget

  • Label the advertising. A free berth given for posts is advertising. The host must use the platform's paid-partnership label and a written "#ad" a reader sees before engaging. Hidden advertising is an unfair commercial practice.
  • State a minimum guest insurance — medical, repatriation, cancellation, personal liability, water sports. "Strongly recommend" is too soft for a commercial charter.
  • The skipper's authority is absolute, in writing, including the right to end a guest's trip without refund for unsafe behaviour.
  • Know who is the carrier. If a licensed operator runs the boat, their name belongs on the contract, the invoice and the insurance — not yours.

Rhythm

Re-verify the country rows and the insurance before the first departure, then annually. Update the Terms and Privacy Policy, re-date them on the website, refresh the safety briefing with last season's near misses, and archive the old trip files.

Templates and checklists only — not legal advice. Have the guest-facing documents reviewed by a lawyer qualified in the law you sell under, and confirm every licensing point with the authority named in the full pack. Nothing here substitutes for a valid charter licence, a valid insurance policy, or the rules of the flag and coastal states.
